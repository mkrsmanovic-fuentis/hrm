The mock API follows these rules so the frontend behaves as it will with the real backend.

## Organizations

- Every record belongs to an organization.
- The MVP has a single organization. There is no organization switcher and no role above Administrator.
- The model supports several organizations later. Data from one organization must never be visible in another.

## Departments

- Administrators create, rename, describe, deactivate and reactivate departments. Employees have no access. See [Access by role](#access-by-role).
- A department has a name of 1 to 100 characters and an optional description of up to 500. Spaces around them are removed.
- The name is unique within the organization, among active and inactive departments, ignoring upper and lower case. "finance" cannot be added next to "Finance".
- A new department is active.
- Departments are deactivated, never deleted. Deactivating keeps the department's history, and its employees keep their department. Reactivating makes it active again, with nothing else changed.
- The employee count of a department is the number of its **active** users, administrators included. Deactivated users are not counted.
- An **inactive** department cannot be chosen for a new or edited user, and cannot be used to assign training by department. Its current members keep it as their department, and it still appears in lists, filters, reports and history.

## Users and employees

Every account is a user: administrators and employees alike. Administrators manage them on the Employees page.

- Administrators create, edit, deactivate and reactivate users, and set their role. Employees have no access. See [Access by role](#access-by-role).
- A user has a first and a last name of 1 to 100 characters each, an email of up to 254 characters, a role, and an optional department. Spaces around the text are removed.
- The email is unique within the organization, among active and inactive users, ignoring upper and lower case and spaces around it. Sign-in matches emails the same way.
- The role is **Administrator** or **Employee**. Administrators can give any other user either role.
- Only an **active** department can be chosen for a new or edited user. A user who is already in a department that was deactivated later keeps it, until they are moved to another department. See [Departments](#departments). A user can also have no department.
- A new user is active and gets an invite link to set a password, valid for **7 days**. Until they set a password, they cannot sign in. See [Accounts and access](#accounts-and-access).
- Users are deactivated, never deleted. Deactivating keeps their history and their training results.
- **Deactivating ends the user's sessions at once.** Their next request gets `401`, and they cannot sign in again. Their open assignments stay unchanged, but are left out of pending and overdue counts.
- **Reactivating** makes the user active again. They can sign in with their password, and their open assignments count as pending and overdue again. Sessions that ended when they were deactivated do not come back.
- **Administrators cannot deactivate themselves or change their own role.** So the organization always keeps at least one active administrator.

### Training counts

Each user has two derived counts, shown in the employee list:

| Count | Field | Includes |
| - | - | - |
| Assigned training | `assignedTrainingCount` | Every assignment the user has received, in any [status](#assignment-status): Not Started, In Progress, Failed, Overdue and Passed. A deactivated user's open assignments still count, because deactivating leaves them unchanged. |
| Completed training | `completedTrainingCount` | The user's assignments with the status **Passed**, including ones passed after the deadline. |

These are totals per user, not pending or overdue counts, so a user's status does not change them. Pending and overdue counts, for example in reports, leave out the open assignments of deactivated users.

## Topics

- Administrators create, edit, deactivate and reactivate topics. Employees read active topics only. See [Access by role](#access-by-role).
- A topic has a name of 1 to 100 characters, a required description of 1 to 1,000 characters and an optional icon. Spaces around the name and description are removed.
- The name is unique within the organization, among active and inactive topics, ignoring upper and lower case and spaces around it. "phishing awareness" cannot be added next to "Phishing Awareness".
- The icon is a Lucide icon name from a curated list of security icons, `TopicIcon` in the [API spec](/api-reference/overview). Any other name is rejected. A topic without an icon has `null`.
- A new topic is active.
- Each questionnaire belongs to exactly one topic. A topic can have many questionnaires.
- The questionnaire count of a topic is the number of **all** its questionnaires, active and inactive. Inactive questionnaires still belong to the topic and keep their results.
- Topics are deactivated, never deleted. Deactivating keeps the topic's history, and the results of its questionnaires stay accessible (FR-014). Reactivating makes it active again, with nothing else changed.
- Deactivating a topic does not change its questionnaires: their status, versions, assignments and attempts stay as they are. Employees no longer see the topic itself.
- An **inactive** topic cannot be chosen for a new questionnaire, and a questionnaire cannot be moved into it.
- Questionnaires in an inactive topic **cannot be assigned**. Existing assignments on them continue and can still be completed, and all history stays.

## Questionnaires

- Administrators create questionnaires, change their settings, and activate and deactivate them. Employees have no questionnaire list; they reach a questionnaire only through their own assignments. See [Access by role](#access-by-role).
- The settings are a title of 1 to 200 characters, an optional description of up to 2,000, a topic, a passing score and an optional time limit. Spaces around the title and description are removed, and an empty description is saved as none.
- Titles are **not** unique. Two questionnaires can have the same title, for example in different topics.
- The passing score is a whole number from 1 to 100. The default is **80**. See [Scoring](#scoring).
- The time limit is a whole number of minutes from 1 to 480 (8 hours, the length of a session), or none. See [Time limits](#time-limits).
- The topic must be **active** when a questionnaire is created, and when a questionnaire moves to another topic. A questionnaire whose topic was deactivated later stays in it, and can still be edited there. See [Topics](#topics).

### Draft first

- A new questionnaire is **inactive**, a draft, with an empty version 1. Questions are added to that version.
- A questionnaire can be activated only when its current version has at least one question. Otherwise activating is refused and nothing changes.
- Activating makes it available to assign. Deactivating stops new assignments; existing assignments can still be completed, and all attempts and results are kept. See [Assigning training](#assigning-training).
- Questionnaires are deactivated, never deleted.

### Settings and versions

- Changing the settings (title, description, topic, passing score, time limit) does **not** create a new version. Versions are about questions. See [Questionnaire versions](#questionnaire-versions).
- Each attempt keeps the passing score it started with. A new passing score applies to attempts started after the change, and never changes earlier results.

## Scoring

The score is the share of correct answers:

```text
Score (%) = (correct answers / total questions) × 100
```

- An attempt **passes** when its exact score is equal to or greater than the questionnaire's passing score. Otherwise it **fails**.
- Scores are shown rounded **down** to a whole percent. For example, 79.6% fails at a passing score of 80% and is shown as 79%.
- Each questionnaire has its own passing score: a whole number from 1 to 100. The default is **80**.
- Every question is required. The employee must answer all of them before submitting.
- A questionnaire counts as completed only after the employee submits it.
- The same attempt cannot be submitted twice.

## Feedback after submitting

- After a **failed** attempt, the employee sees which questions were wrong and the feedback for them, but not the correct answers.
- After **passing**, the employee also sees the correct answers.

## Assignment status

Each assignment has one status, evaluated in this order:

| Order | Status | When |
| - | - | - |
| 1 | Passed | Any attempt passed, even after the deadline. |
| 2 | Overdue | The deadline has passed. |
| 3 | Failed | The latest attempt failed. |
| 3 | In Progress | An attempt is started but not submitted. |
| 3 | Not Started | No attempts yet. |

Employees can still complete overdue training. Reports show the completion date, so late completions are visible.

## Attempts and retakes

- An assignment has at most one open attempt. Without a time limit, an unfinished attempt stays open and the employee can continue it.
- Employees can retake a failed questionnaire. There is no limit on attempts.
- Each retake creates a new attempt. Earlier attempts and scores are kept.
- Failed attempts stay available for administrators to review.
- Once an assignment is passed, it cannot be retaken.

## Time limits

- A questionnaire can have an optional time limit.
- When time runs out, the attempt is submitted automatically. Unanswered questions count as wrong.

## Assigning training

- A questionnaire can be assigned to one employee, several employees or a whole department.
- Assigning to a department creates an assignment for every **active** employee in it at that moment. Employees who join later are not included.
- Inactive questionnaires cannot be assigned. Deactivating a questionnaire does not affect existing assignments; they can still be completed.
- A questionnaire cannot be assigned to an employee who already has an open assignment for it.
- Assigning again after a pass creates a new assignment. The earlier one stays in the history.
- There is no automatic recurring training. Administrators assign training again by hand.

## Notifications

- Employees are notified in the app when training is assigned to them.
- Employees are reminded 3 days before a deadline, unless they have already passed.
- Email sends the same two messages. This is planned as an enhancement.

## Accounts and access

- There is one Administrator role with full access, and one Employee role.
- Employees can only see their own assignments and results, and cannot use administrator functions. See [Access by role](#access-by-role).
- Users log in with email and password. Passwords are stored securely hashed.
- Creating an employee sends an invite link to set a password, valid for **7 days**. Administrators can send it again.
- A password reset link is valid for **1 hour**.
- Deactivated employees cannot log in, and deactivating ends their sessions at once. Their open assignments stay unchanged but are left out of pending and overdue counts. Reactivating the employee brings them back. See [Users and employees](#users-and-employees).
- All communication uses HTTPS.

## Sign-in and sessions

- Users sign in with their email and password. The email is matched without regard to upper and lower case, and spaces around it are ignored.
- Only **active** users who have **set a password** can sign in. Users with a pending or expired invite have no password yet.
- A wrong password, an unknown email and a user without a password get the same answer, so sign-in does not reveal which emails have accounts.
- A deactivated user who enters the right password is told the account is deactivated.
- A session lasts **8 hours** from sign-in. Activity does not extend it. After that, the user must sign in again.
- Signing out ends the session at once. Its token stops working.
- Deactivating a user ends all their sessions at once: their next request gets `401`.
- Every endpoint except signing in and signing out needs a valid session token.

The real backend must also:

- Store passwords only as a secure, salted hash made with a slow password-hashing algorithm such as Argon2id, bcrypt or scrypt. Never store or log passwords in plain text, and never send them back in a response.
- Serve the app and the API only over HTTPS, and redirect plain HTTP to HTTPS. Session tokens and passwords must never travel unencrypted.
- Generate session tokens with a cryptographically secure random generator, so they cannot be guessed.

The mock API stores no passwords: every seeded user who has set one signs in with the same demo password. See [Authentication](/developer-guide/authentication#demo-accounts).

## Access by role

Every user has exactly one role: **Administrator** or **Employee**. The role comes from the session, never from the request. Administrators have full access to their organization's data. Employees see only their own training and the content it needs.

### Access matrix

What each role can do in each module. "Own" means records that belong to the signed-in user, for example assignments whose `userId` is theirs.

| Module | Administrator | Employee |
| - | - | - |
| Sign-in and session | Sign in, read the current session, sign out. | Sign in, read the current session, sign out. |
| Organization | Read the current organization. | Read the current organization. |
| Employees | List and read every user. Create, edit, deactivate and reactivate users, and set their role, except their own role and their own deactivation. Send the invite link again. | Read only their own user record, through the current session (`GET /auth/session`). No employee list, and no other users. |
| Departments | List, read, create, edit, deactivate and reactivate departments. | No access. |
| Topics | List and read every topic, active and inactive. Create, edit, deactivate and reactivate topics. | List and read **active** topics only. An inactive topic answers as if it did not exist. |
| Questionnaires, questions and versions | List and read every questionnaire, with its questions, answer options and versions. Create, edit, activate, deactivate and preview questionnaires. | Read only questionnaires assigned to them, through their own assignments. No questionnaire list. Correct answers only as [Feedback after submitting](#feedback-after-submitting) allows. |
| Assignments | List and read every assignment. Assign questionnaires to employees and departments, with deadlines. | List and read their own assignments only. |
| My training: attempts and results | List and read every attempt and result, including failed attempts. | Start, answer and submit attempts on their own assignments, retake failed ones, and read their own results, feedback and history. |
| Reports and dashboards | The training overview, topic, department, employee and questionnaire reports, filters and CSV export. | No access. Their own progress appears on their own dashboard. |
| Notifications | No notifications in the MVP. | Read their own notifications and mark them as read. |
| Audit log | Their data changes are logged automatically. No screen or endpoint to read the log in the MVP. | No access. |

- Employees still see their past assignments and results on a topic or questionnaire that was deactivated later, through their own assignments and history. See [Data integrity](#data-integrity).
- Only employees take training. The app gives administrators no My training area.

### How the backend enforces it

- **Administrator-only endpoints** answer `403` with type `/problems/forbidden` when an employee calls them. The check comes first: before validating the request and before reading or changing data.
- **Own data only:** list endpoints return only the employee's own records. A single record that belongs to another user answers `404`, the same as a record that does not exist, so employees cannot find out which IDs exist.
- **Content an employee may not see**, such as an inactive topic or a questionnaire not assigned to them, also answers `404`, and is left out of lists.
- A `403` never ends the session. The user stays signed in.
- The frontend hides pages and navigation entries from the wrong role, but that is only for convenience. The backend must enforce every rule above on every endpoint.
- Access is always within the user's organization. See [Organizations](#organizations).

The [API reference](/api-reference/overview#authorization) describes the answers. [Roles and access](/developer-guide/access-control) shows how the frontend and the mock apply these rules.

## Questionnaire versions

- A questionnaire with no attempts is edited in place.
- Editing the questions of a questionnaire that has attempts creates a new version. Changing its settings does not.
- New attempts use the latest version. An attempt in progress finishes on the version it started with.
- Each attempt stays linked to the version it used, so earlier results never change.

## Data integrity

- Submitted attempts are never changed or deleted.
- Deactivating an employee, department, topic or questionnaire keeps its history.

## Audit log

Administrator actions that change data are logged, so it is always clear who did what and when.

### Logged actions

| Record | `entityType` | Logged actions |
| - | - | - |
| Employee | `user` | `created`, `updated`, `deactivated`, `reactivated` |
| Department | `department` | `created`, `updated`, `deactivated`, `reactivated` |
| Topic | `topic` | `created`, `updated`, `deactivated`, `reactivated` |
| Questionnaire | `questionnaire` | `created`, `updated`, `deactivated`, `reactivated` |
| Assignment | `assignment` | `created` |

- Assigning a questionnaire to several employees or a whole department logs one `created` entry for each assignment it creates.
- A new questionnaire is logged as `created` with `status` `inactive`. Activating it, the first time or later, is logged as `reactivated`.
- Editing a questionnaire that creates a new version is logged as `updated` on the questionnaire. The entry lists the new `currentVersionId` and any other changed fields.
- A change of `status` is always logged as `deactivated` or `reactivated`, never as `updated`, even when it arrives in an edit request. If the same edit also changes other fields, it logs two entries: `updated` with the other fields, then `deactivated` or `reactivated`.
- Only successful actions are logged. A request that fails, for example with a validation error, logs nothing.
- Employee actions, such as starting and submitting attempts, are not logged.

### What each entry records

Each entry is an `AuditLogEntry` in the [API spec](/api-reference/overview). See the [Data model](/developer-guide/data-model#audit-log) for its fields.

| What | Field | Rule |
| - | - | - |
| Who | `actorId` | The signed-in administrator, taken from the session. |
| What | `action`, `entityType`, `entityId` | The action and the record it was done to. |
| What changed | `changes` | A list of `{ field, oldValue, newValue }`, described below. |
| When | `createdAt` | The time of the action, in UTC. |

The `changes` list depends on the action:

- **`created`:** every field the new record has a value for. `oldValue` is `null`.
- **`updated`:** only the fields whose value changed, with the value before and after. An edit that changes no field is not logged.
- **`deactivated`** and **`reactivated`:** empty. The action says what happened.

In `changes`:

- Field names and values are the ones in the API spec, for example `departmentId` with a department ID.
- `id`, `organizationId`, `createdAt` and `updatedAt` are never listed. Neither are *derived* fields, such as `questionCount`.
- A field without a value is `null`.

### Keeping the log

- An entry is written together with the change it records. If the change is not saved, the entry is not either.
- Entries are never changed or deleted. `updatedAt` always equals `createdAt`.
- Entries belong to the organization of the record, like every other record.
- There is no screen and no API endpoint for viewing the audit log in the MVP.
